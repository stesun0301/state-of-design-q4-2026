What people told us

The qualitative picture is mixed but constructive: people recognise better tools and connection, while the structural conditions for quality still feel uneven.

Quality is not consistently defined or defended

Late involvement and inconsistent cross-functional expectations make quality the easiest trade-off under pressure.

“Because there’s no shared definition, quality becomes the easiest thing to drop when something has to give.”

Capacity and planning create rework

Staffing gaps, changing priorities and delivery pressure reduce time for validation and fixing underlying causes.

“Structural problems cause work to be inefficient, but inefficient work means there’s no time to work on resolving structural problems.”

Visibility is improving, but silos persist

Design-wide communication feels stronger, while product bubbles and inconsistent daily sharing remain.

“The way of working is still heavily in the individual teams … but at least the feel of being part of a big design group is there.”

AI is widespread and useful, but capability is uneven

Use spans research, prototyping, workflows and code contribution. Some people still need time, budgets and clearer contribution routes.

“Claude code and integrations significantly helped … some things currently take WAY less than in the beginning of the year.”

Where to focus in 2027

Three priorities grounded in the strongest quantitative and qualitative signals.

01

Align how Product, Engineering and Design make quality trade-offs

Define a practical quality bar, decision rights and escalation path for moments where delivery pressure conflicts with quality.

02

Tackle capacity, planning and prioritisation together

Review portfolio load, planning assumptions, scope changes and research/validation time as one system—not separate execution problems.

03

Turn local practices into reusable approaches

Trial field-tested practices such as early technical/design reviews, public priorities, Design PRs, demos and AI-sharing across additional teams.

Method and limitations
Data source: anonymous Q1 and Q4 2026 State of Design survey exports. Moderate and significant ratings are combined. New Q4 measures—capacity, planning, visibility and access to insights—are presented as baselines, not Q1 comparisons. Small function-level groups are not used for quantitative comparisons and quotes are anonymised.
